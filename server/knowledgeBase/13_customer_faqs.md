# Dubai Real Estate – Frequently Asked Questions (Customer FAQs)

## 1. Foreign Ownership & Non-Resident Purchasing

### Can a foreign citizen or non-resident buy property in Dubai?
**Yes, absolutely.** Under Dubai Law No. 7 of 2006, foreign nationals of any nationality (both residents and non-residents) are permitted to purchase real estate on a **100% freehold** basis in designated freehold areas across Dubai. You do not need a UAE residence visa to purchase or own property.

### Can I purchase property remotely from overseas without visiting Dubai?
**Yes.** Modern Dubai real estate transactions can be executed completely remotely:
- **Off-Plan Purchases:** Can be completed via developer digital reservation agreements and direct international bank transfers into RERA-monitored project Escrow accounts.
- **Secondary Market Purchases:** Can be completed by granting a legal **Power of Attorney (POA)** to an authorized lawyer, conveyancer, or trusted representative. POAs executed outside the UAE can be notarized in your home country, attested by the UAE Embassy and MOFA, or drafted via UAE digital remote notary services.
- **DLD Digital Conveyance:** The Dubai Land Department also supports remote digital transfers using video identity verification and OTP authentication.

## 2. Joint Ownership, Co-Buying, and Corporate Entities

### Can multiple individuals buy property jointly?
**Yes.** Spouses, family members, or business partners can purchase property jointly. The DLD Title Deed explicitly records each owner's legal percentage share (e.g., 50/50, 60/40).

### Can an offshore or foreign company own property in Dubai?
Foreign offshore entities cannot directly hold Dubai real estate unless registered through **JAFZA Offshore** (Jebel Ali Free Zone) or designated approved free zones (such as DIFC or ADGM) that have bilateral registration agreements with the Dubai Land Department.

## 3. Funds Transfer, Currencies, and Capital Repatriation

### How are payments transferred securely to Dubai?
- **Off-Plan:** Funds are wired via international SWIFT transfer directly into the project's **RERA Escrow Account** held with an authorized UAE bank. Payments are protected by UAE Law No. 8 of 2007.
- **Secondary Market:** Funds are transferred to the buyer's UAE bank account or a regulated conveyancer's client account, where a **Manager's Cheque** is issued payable to the seller and Dubai Land Department.
- **Repatriation:** Dubai imposes **zero capital controls**, zero currency limits, and zero restrictions on repatriating sales proceeds, dividends, or rental yields back to your home country.

## 4. Taxes and Ongoing Property Holding Costs

### Are there property taxes, rental income taxes, or capital gains taxes in Dubai?
**No.** Dubai real estate is free from:
- **0% Personal Income Tax** on rental revenue.
- **0% Capital Gains Tax** upon property resale.
- **0% Annual Property Holding Tax / Municipal Wealth Tax.**
*Note:* The only mandatory fees are the one-time **4% DLD transfer fee** at purchase and standard annual building service charges regulated by RERA Mollak. (Commercial leases and commercial property transfers carry 5% VAT; residential resales are VAT-exempt).

## 5. Utilities Setup & Maintenance Responsibilities

### How are water, electricity, and air conditioning connected?
- **DEWA (Dubai Electricity & Water Authority):** When an Ejari tenancy or Title Deed is registered, DEWA setup is initiated automatically through digital system integration.
  - Refundable Security Deposit: **AED 2,000** for apartments / **AED 4,000** for villas.
  - Move-in activation is typically completed within 15 to 24 hours.
- **District Cooling (Empower, Tabreed, Emicool):** If the building utilizes district cooling, the tenant/owner registers an account directly with the cooling provider and deposits a refundable utility bond (typically AED 1,000 to AED 2,000).

### Who pays for property maintenance during a tenancy?
Under standard RERA guidelines and tenancy contracts:
- **Minor Maintenance (under AED 500 or AED 1,000 per incident):** Covered by the **tenant** (e.g., lightbulbs, minor plumbing leaks, drain clearing).
- **Major Repairs (exceeding AED 500 or AED 1,000):** Legally the **landlord’s responsibility** (e.g., central A/C compressor replacement, water heater renewal, structural repairs, major pipework).

## 6. Wills and Non-Muslim Inheritance

### What happens to Dubai property if a foreign non-Muslim owner passes away?
Non-Muslim expatriates and international investors can register a formal Will with the **DIFC Wills Service Centre** or **Dubai Courts**. A registered DIFC Will guarantees that the deceased investor's Dubai real estate assets are distributed strictly in accordance with their designated beneficiaries, bypassing standard Sharia default inheritance rules.
